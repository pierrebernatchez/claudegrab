Part 1: Reference Angles
================================================================================

.. rst-class:: solution

**Initial Arm:**

Always lies on the positive :math:`x`-axis at 0°. Meets the terminal arm
at the origin.

.. rst-class:: solution

**Terminal Arm:**

The arm that rotates around the origin counter clockwise to form a
positive angle or clockwise for a negative angle.

:sol:`Angle` :solmath:`\theta` is measured from the initial arm to the
terminal arm.

.. image:: ../images/mau5lesson02-gpimage01.png
   :scale: 70

.. rst-class:: solution

**Principal Angle:**

The counter clockwise angle between the initial arm and the terminal arm
of an angle in standard position. It's value is between 0° and 360°.

.. rst-class:: solution

**Related Acute Angle (reference angle):**

The acute angle between the terminal arm of an angle in standard
position and the closest :math:`x`-axis when the terminal arm lies in
quadrant 2, 3, or 4.

The reference angle helps us determine the exact trig ratios when we are
given obtuse angles.

.. image:: ../images/mau5lesson02-gpimage02.png
   :scale: 70

Part 2: Evaluating Trig Ratios for Any Angle
================================================================================

For any point :math:`P(x,y)` in the Cartesian plane, the trigonometric
ratios for angles in standard position can be expressed in terms of
:math:`x`, :math:`y`, and :math:`r`.

:math:`\sin\theta =` :solmath:`\dfrac{y}{r}`

:math:`\cos\theta =` :solmath:`\dfrac{x}{r}`

:math:`\tan\theta =` :solmath:`\dfrac{y}{x}`

.. image:: ../images/mau5lesson02-gpimage07.png
   :scale: 65

The CAST rule is an easy way to remember which primary trig ratios are
positive in which quadrant. Since :math:`r` is always positive, the sign
of each primary ratio depends on the signs of the coordinates of the
point :math:`(x,y)`.

In Q1,

.. rst-class:: solution

**All**

ratios are positive because both :math:`x` and :math:`y` are positive.

In Q2, only

.. rst-class:: solution

**Sine**

is positive, since :math:`x` is negative and :math:`y` is positive.

In Q3, only

.. rst-class:: solution

**Tangent**

is positive, since both :math:`x` and :math:`y` are negative.

In Q4, only

.. rst-class:: solution

**Cosine**

is positive, since :math:`x` is positive but :math:`y` is negative.

   :scale: 70

Part 3: Unit Circle
================================================================================

The unit circle, a circle with a radius of **1 unit**, is very useful
since the :math:`x` and :math:`y` coordinates of where the terminal
intersects it tell us the Cosine and Sine ratios respectively.

For any point :math:`P(x,y)` in the Cartesian plane that intersects the
*unit circle*, the trigonometric ratios for angles can be expressed in
terms of :math:`x`, :math:`y`, and :math:`r`.

:math:`\sin\theta =` :solmath:`\dfrac{y}{1} = y`

:math:`\cos\theta =` :solmath:`\dfrac{x}{1} = x`

:math:`\tan\theta =` :solmath:`\dfrac{y}{x}`

Part 4: Negative and Co-terminal Angles
================================================================================

Co-terminal angles are angles in standard position that have the

.. rst-class:: solution

**same terminal arm**.

Starting at 30° and rotating 360° counter clockwise will bring you back
to the same terminal arm.

:math:`30\degree + 360\degree = 390\degree`

Therefore, 30° and 390° are co-terminal.

.. image:: ../images/mau5lesson02-gpimage18.png
   :scale: 70

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

**Example 5:** Find three co-terminal angles of 60°

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\theta_1 = 60\degree + 360\degree \Rightarrow \theta_1 = 420\degree`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\theta_2 = 60\degree + 2(360\degree) \Rightarrow \theta_2 = 780\degree`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\theta_3 = 60\degree + 3(360\degree) \Rightarrow \theta_3 = 1140\degree`

A negative angle is an angle measured

.. rst-class:: solution

**clockwise**

from the positive :math:`x`-axis.

You can find an equivalent (co-terminal) positive angle by adding 360°
to the negative angle.

:math:`-210\degree` and :math:`150\degree` have the same terminal arm
(co-terminal) and therefore have the same trigonometric ratios.

   :scale: 70
