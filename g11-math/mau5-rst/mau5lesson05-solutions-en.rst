:sol:`Primary trig ratios` are used to solve triangles that contain
a right angle.

.. image:: ../images/mau5lesson05-gpimage01.png
   :scale: 95

The :sol:`Sine Law` and :sol:`Cosine Law` are used to solve
oblique triangles. An oblique triangle is any triangle that is NOT a
right triangle.

Sine Law can be used if you know:

i\) 2 sides and one angle opposite a given side
ii\) 2 angles and any side

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin A} = \dfrac{c}{\sin C}`

The Cosine Law can be used if you know:

i\) 2 sides and the angle contained by those 2 sides
ii\) All 3 sides

.. image:: ../images/mau5lesson05-gpimage02.png
   :scale: 95

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc(\cos A)`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\cos A = \dfrac{a^2-b^2-c^2}{-2bc}`

Part 1: Problems in 2 Dimensions
================================================================================

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

**Example 1:** Jonathan needs a new rope for his flagpole but is unsure
of the length required. He measures a distance of 10m away from the base
of the pole. From this point, the angle of elevation to the top of the
pole is 42°. What is the height of the pole, to the nearest tenth of a
meter?

.. image:: ../images/mau5lesson05-gpimage03.png
   :scale: 95

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\tan(42\degree) = \dfrac{h}{10}`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`h = 10\tan(42\degree)`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`h \approx 9.0` m

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

**Example 2:** Pam, Steven and Rachel are standing on a soccer field.
Steven and Rachel are 23m apart. From Steven's point of view, the other
two are separated by 72°. From Pam's point of view, the others are
separated by an angle of 55°. Determine the distance from Pam to Rachel.

.. image:: ../images/mau5lesson05-gpimage04.png
   :scale: 95

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\dfrac{s}{\sin P} = \dfrac{p}{\sin S}`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\dfrac{23}{\sin(55\degree)} = \dfrac{s}{\sin(72\degree)}`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`s = \dfrac{23\sin(72\degree)}{\sin(55\degree)}`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`s \approx 26.7` m

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

**Example 3:** A drive belt wraps around three pulleys as shown. Find
the perimeter of the drive belt to the nearest tenth of a cm.

.. image:: ../images/mau5lesson05-gpimage05.png
   :scale: 95

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`x^2 = 14.5^2 + 16^2 - 2(14.5)(16)\cos(35\degree)`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`x^2 \approx 86.1634515`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`x \approx 9.3` cm

.. rst-class:: solution

Perimeter :math:`= 9.3 + 14.5 + 16`

.. rst-class:: solution

Perimeter :math:`\approx 39.8` cm

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

**Example 4:** Find the measure of angle G

.. image:: ../images/mau5lesson05-gpimage06.png
   :scale: 95

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\cos G = \dfrac{4.8^2-5.1^2-6.2^2}{-2(5.1)(6.2)}`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\cos G \approx \dfrac{-41.41}{-63.24}`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\angle G = \cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{41.41}{63.24}\right)`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\angle G \approx 49.1\degree`

Part 2: Problems in 3 Dimensions
================================================================================

.. container:: keeptogether

   **Example 4:** A vertical flag pole TP stands in the corner of a
   rectangular field QRST. Using the information given in the diagram,
   calculate (a) The height of the flag pole and (b) The angle of
   elevation of P from S. Round answers to nearest tenth.

   .. image:: ../images/mau5lesson05-gpimage07.png
      :scale: 95

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

a\) :math:`\tan(34\degree) = \dfrac{q}{30}`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`q = 30\tan(34\degree)`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`q \approx 20.2` m

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

b\) :math:`\tan\theta = \dfrac{20.2}{15}`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{20.2}{15}\right)`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\theta \approx 53.4\degree`

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

**Example 5:** From point B, Manny estimates the angle of elevation to
the top of a cliff as 38°. From point D, 68.5 meters away from Manny,
Joe estimates the angle between the base of the cliff, himself, and
Manny to be 42°, while Manny estimates the angle between the base of the
cliff, himself, and his friend Joe to be 63°. What is the height of the
cliff to the nearest tenth of a meter?

.. image:: ../images/mau5lesson05-gpimage08.png
   :scale: 95

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\angle BCD = 180\degree - 63\degree - 42\degree = 75\degree`

.. rst-class:: solution

First, use the Sine Law in triangle BCD to find :math:`x` (the distance
from C to B):

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\dfrac{x}{\sin(42\degree)} = \dfrac{68.5}{\sin(75\degree)}`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`x = \dfrac{68.5\sin(42\degree)}{\sin(75\degree)} \approx 47.45`

.. rst-class:: solution

Then, in the right triangle ABC:

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\tan(38\degree) = \dfrac{h}{47.45}`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`h = 47.45\tan(38\degree)`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`h \approx 37.1` m

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

**Example 6:** Emma is on a 50 meter high bridge and sees two boats
anchored below. From her position, boat A has a bearing of 230° and
boat B has a bearing of 120°. Emma estimates the angles of depression to
be 38° for boat A and 35° for boat B. How far apart are the boats to the
nearest meter?

.. image:: ../images/mau5lesson05-gpimage09.png
   :scale: 95

.. rst-class:: solution

The angle between the two bearings (230° and 120°) measured through
north is :math:`360\degree - 230\degree + 120\degree = 250\degree`, so
the angle :math:`\angle AEB` on the triangle side is
:math:`360\degree - 250\degree = 110\degree`.

.. rst-class:: solution

In the right triangle formed by the bridge, point E, and boat A:
:math:`\tan(38\degree) = \dfrac{50}{x} \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{50}{\tan(38\degree)} \approx 64` m

.. rst-class:: solution

In the right triangle formed by the bridge, point E, and boat B:
:math:`\tan(35\degree) = \dfrac{50}{y} \Rightarrow y = \dfrac{50}{\tan(35\degree)} \approx 71.4` m

.. rst-class:: solution

Using the Cosine Law in triangle AEB (with :math:`EA \approx 64`,
:math:`EB \approx 71.4`, and the contained angle :math:`110\degree`):

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`q^2 = 64^2 + 71.4^2 - 2(64)(71.4)\cos(110\degree)`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`q^2 \approx 12319.75049`

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`q \approx 111` m
