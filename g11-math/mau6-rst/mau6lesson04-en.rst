Section 1: How to Determine the Equation of a Sine or Cosine Function Given its Graph
================================================================================

To determine the equation of a transformed sine or cosine function, you
must use the graph to determine the value of the parameters :math:`a, k,
d,` and :math:`c` to then write the transformed equation in the format:

:math:`y = a\sin[k(x-d)]+c` OR :math:`y = a\cos[k(x-d)]+c`

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :width: 100%

   * - :math:`a`
     - :math:`k`
     - :math:`d`
     - :math:`c`
   * - Find the amplitude of the function:

       :math:`a = \dfrac{y_{max}-y_{min}}{2}`
     - Find the period (in degrees) of the function using a starting
       point and ending point of a full cycle.

       :math:`k = \dfrac{360}{\text{period}}`
     - for :math:`\sin x`: x-coordinate of a rising mid-line.

       for :math:`\cos x`: x-coordinate of a maximum point.

       :math:`d_{sin} = d_{cos} - \dfrac{90}{|k|}`

       :math:`d_{cos} = d_{sin} + \dfrac{90}{|k|}`
     - Find the vertical shift

       :math:`c = max - amplitude`

       OR

       :math:`c = \dfrac{max+min}{2}` (this finds the 'middle' of the
       function)

**Example 2:** A sinusoidal function has an amplitude of 3 units, a
period of 180 degrees and a max point at (0, 5). Represent the function
with an equation in two different ways.

.. image:: ../images/mau6lesson04-gpimage04.png
   :scale: 100

*Note: the x-value of the maximum point was not obvious from the graph.
You need to know that maximum points are always* :math:`\frac{90}{|k|}`
*to the right of the rising midline point. Also, if you knew where the
maximum point was, the rising midline point would be* :math:`\frac{90}
{|k|}` *to the left of the max.*

:math:`d_{cos} = d_{sin}+\dfrac{90}{|k|}` OR :math:`d_{sin} =
d_{cos}-\dfrac{90}{|k|}`
