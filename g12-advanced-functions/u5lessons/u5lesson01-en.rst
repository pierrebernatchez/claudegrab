   * - :math:`\csc\theta = \dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}`

       :math:`\sec\theta = \dfrac{1}{\cos\theta}`

       :math:`\cot\theta = \dfrac{1}{\tan\theta}`

.. list-table:: Tips and Tricks
   :widths: 33 33 34
   :width: 100%
   :header-rows: 1

   * - Reciprocal Identities
     - Quotient Identities
     - Pythagorean Identities
   * - Square both sides

       :math:`\csc^2\theta = \dfrac{1}{\sin^2\theta}`

       :math:`\sec^2\theta = \dfrac{1}{\cos^2\theta}`

       :math:`\cot^2\theta = \dfrac{1}{\tan^2\theta}`
     - Square both sides

       :math:`\dfrac{\sin^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta} = \tan^2\theta`

       :math:`\dfrac{\cos^2\theta}{\sin^2\theta} = \cot^2\theta`
     - Rearrange the identity

       :math:`\sin^2\theta = 1 - \cos^2\theta`

       :math:`\cos^2\theta = 1 - \sin^2\theta`

       Divide by either :math:`\sin^2\theta` or :math:`\cos^2\theta`

       :math:`1 + \cot^2\theta = \csc^2\theta`

       :math:`\tan^2\theta + 1 = \sec^2\theta`

General tips for proving identities:

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

i\) Separate into LS and RS. Terms may NOT cross between sides.

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

ii\) Try to change everything to :math:`\sin\theta` or :math:`\cos\theta`

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

iii\) If you have two fractions being added or subtracted, find a common
denominator and combine the fractions.

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

iv\) Use difference of squares :math:`\rightarrow 1 - \sin^2\theta = (1-\sin\theta)(1+\sin\theta)`

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

v\) Use the power rule :math:`\rightarrow \sin^6\theta = (\sin^2\theta)^3`

Part 2: Transformation Identities
================================================================================

Because of their periodic nature, there are many equivalent trigonometric
expressions.

Horizontal translations of ``_____`` that involve both a sine function and
a cosine function can be used to obtain two equivalent functions with the
same graph.

Translating the cosine function :math:`\pi/2` to the
``________________________``, :math:`f(x) = \cos\left(x - \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)`
results in the graph of the sine function, :math:`f(x) = \sin x`.

Similarly, translating the sine function :math:`\pi/2` to the
``________________``, :math:`f(x) = \sin\left(x + \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)`
results in the graph of the cosine function, :math:`f(x) = \cos x`.
