Resolving a Vector into its Components
================================================================================

In many situations involving forces, we are interested in a process
that is the opposite of composition. This process is called
:sol:`RESOLUTION`, which means taking a single force and decomposing
it into two components. When we resolve a force into two components,
it is possible to do this in an infinite number of ways because there
are infinitely many parallelograms having a particular single force as
the diagonal. However, the most useful and important way to resolve a
force vector occurs when this vector is resolved into two components
that are at right angles to each other. These components are usually
referred to as the horizontal and vertical components or
:sol:`RECTANGULAR VECTOR COMPONENTS`.

.. list-table::
   :widths: 50 50
   :header-rows: 0
   :width: 100%

   * - If :math:`\vec{f}` is resolved into its respective horizontal
       and vertical components, :math:`\vec{f_x}` and
       :math:`\vec{f_y}`, then:

       :solmath:`|\vec{f_x}| = |\vec{f}| \cos\theta`

       :solmath:`|\vec{f_y}| = |\vec{f}| \sin\theta`

       Where :math:`\theta` is the angle that :math:`\vec{f}` makes
       with the :math:`x`-axis.
     - .. image:: ../images/cvu4lesson05-gpimage01.png
          :scale: 85
          :alt: vector f resolved into horizontal component fx in red and vertical component fy in blue

**Example 1:** A tow truck is pulling a car from a ditch. The tension
in the cable is 15 000 N at an angle of 40° to the horizontal.

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

a\) Draw a diagram showing the resolution of the force into its
rectangular vector components.

.. rst-class:: solution

.. image:: ../images/cvu4lesson05-gpimage02.png
   :scale: 85
   :alt: 15 000 N force at 40 degrees resolved into fx in red and fy in blue

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

b\) Determine the magnitudes of the horizontal and vertical vector
components of the force.

.. list-table::
   :widths: 50 50
   :header-rows: 0
   :width: 100%

   * - :solmath:`|\vec{f_x}| = 15000 \cos 40`

       :solmath:`|\vec{f_x}| \approx 11490.7` N
     - :solmath:`|\vec{f_y}| = 15000 \sin 40`

       :solmath:`|\vec{f_y}| \approx 9641.8` N

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

**Example 2:** Kayla pulls on a rope attached to her sleigh with a
force of 200 N. If the rope makes an angle of 20° with the horizontal,
determine:

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

a\) the force that pulls the sleigh forward :math:`= |\vec{f_x}|`

.. list-table::
   :widths: 60 40
   :header-rows: 0
   :width: 100%

   * - :solmath:`|\vec{f_x}| = 200 \cos 20`

       :solmath:`|\vec{f_x}| \approx 187.9` N

       b\) the force that tends to lift the sleigh :math:`= |\vec{f_y}|`

       :solmath:`|\vec{f_y}| = 200 \sin 20`

       :solmath:`|\vec{f_y}| \approx 68.4` N
     - .. image:: ../images/cvu4lesson05-gpimage03.png
          :scale: 85
          :alt: 200 N force at 20 degrees resolved into fx in red and fy in blue

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

**Example 3:** A box weighing 140 N is resting on a ramp that is
inclined at an angle of 20°. Resolve the weight into rectangular
vector components that keep the box at rest.

.. list-table::
   :widths: 50 50
   :header-rows: 1
   :width: 100%

   * - Position Diagram
     - Vector Diagram
   * - .. image:: ../images/cvu4lesson05-gpimage04.png
          :scale: 85
     - .. image:: ../images/cvu4lesson05-gpimage05.png
          :scale: 85

The force acting perpendicular to the ramp, :math:`\vec{n}`, and the
force of friction on the ramp, :math:`\vec{f}`, are holding the box in
a state of equilibrium. Therefore, the resultant must be equal to the
opposite of the force of the box.

.. list-table::
   :widths: 50 50
   :header-rows: 0
   :width: 100%

   * - :solmath:`|\vec{n}| = 140 \cos 20`

       :solmath:`|\vec{n}| \approx 131.6` N
     - :solmath:`|\vec{f}| = 140 \sin 20`

       :solmath:`|\vec{f}| \approx 47.9` N

.. rst-class:: solution

The box is kept at rest by a force of 131.6 N acting perpendicular to
the ramp and by friction of 47.9 N parallel to the surface of the
ramp.
