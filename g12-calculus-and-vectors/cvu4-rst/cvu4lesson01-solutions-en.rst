Part 1: What is a Vector?
================================================================================

A :sol:`SCALAR` quantity describes magnitude or size only. It does NOT
include a direction.

Examples: temperature (:math:`-5°\text{C}`), distance (5 km), speed
(100 km/h), mass (10 kg)

A :sol:`VECTOR` is a mathematical quantity having both MAGNITUDE and
DIRECTION

Examples: velocity (80 km/h west), force (10 N downward)

Vectors are represented with directed line segments. A directed line
segment has a :sol:`length`, called its magnitude, and a
:sol:`direction` indicated by an arrowhead.

.. image:: ../images/cvu4lesson01-gpimage01.png
   :scale: 85
   :alt: vector v from tail A to head B

Vector :math:`\vec{AB}` has a starting point at A and ends at point B.
It could also be expressed using a single letter :math:`\vec{v}`

The magnitude, or size, of a vector is designated using absolute value
brackets. The magnitude of vector :math:`\vec{AB}` or :math:`\vec{v}`
is written as :math:`|\vec{AB}|` or :math:`|\vec{v}|`. Magnitude is
always a non-negative value.

A vector's direction can be expressed in several different ways:

Part 2: Equivalent and Opposite Vectors
================================================================================

**Parallel Vectors:** Vectors that have the same OR opposite direction,
but not necessarily the same magnitude.

:math:`\vec{AB} \parallel \vec{DC}`

And

:math:`\vec{AB} \parallel \vec{CD}`

.. image:: ../images/cvu4lesson01-gpimage10.png
   :scale: 85
   :alt: trapezoid ABCD with parallel sides AB and CD marked with arrowheads

**Equivalent Vectors:** Vectors that have the same magnitude AND
direction. The location of the vectors does NOT matter.

Notice that any of these vectors could be translated to be coincident
with either of the other two.

:math:`\vec{p} = \vec{q} = \vec{r}`

Or

:math:`\vec{AB} = \vec{CD} = \vec{EF}`

.. image:: ../images/cvu4lesson01-gpimage11.png
   :scale: 85
   :alt: three equivalent parallel vectors p, q, r at staggered heights

**Opposite Vectors:** Vectors that have the same magnitude but point in
opposite directions.

.. image:: ../images/cvu4lesson01-gpimage12.png
   :scale: 85
   :alt: two opposite horizontal vectors v and -v between the same two points A and B

Notice that :math:`|\vec{AB}| = |\vec{BA}|` but they point in opposite
directions. Therefore :math:`\vec{AB} \neq \vec{BA}`.

You can write an expression for an opposite vector by placing a
negative sign in front of it or by reversing the order of the letters.
The opposite of :math:`\vec{AB}` can be written as :math:`-\vec{AB}`
or :math:`\vec{BA}`

An equivalent expression between the two vectors shown could be
:math:`\vec{AB} = -\vec{BA}`

.. rst-class:: keepwithnext

**Example 2:** Given :math:`\vec{AB}`, draw an equivalent vector
:math:`\vec{CD}` and an opposite vector :math:`\vec{EF}`. Write
equations to show the relationship between the vectors.

.. image:: ../images/cvu4lesson01-gpimage13.png
   :scale: 85
   :alt: vector AB in black, equivalent vector CD in red parallel and same direction, opposite vector EF in blue parallel but opposite direction

.. rst-class:: solution

:math:`\vec{AB} = \vec{CD}`

:math:`\vec{AB} = -\vec{EF}`
